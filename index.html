Ang adbokasiya

Every commuter deserves a ride that works.

Every day, millions of Filipinos get to school, work and home on jeepneys, buses, trains, UV Express and tricycles. Para Po Commuters asks that the people who ride come first when decisions are made about roads, routes and fares.

Why “Para po”? Commuters say it to ask the driver to stop. Para sa commuter means “for the commuter.” We use both meanings. Stop what is not working, and start working for the people who ride.

What we stand for

  • Ligtas

    Safe. Lit streets and stops, roadworthy vehicles, trained drivers, and security in stations and on board, especially for women, students and night-shift workers.

  • Abot-kaya

    Affordable. Fares that fit a daily wage. Student, senior and PWD discounts are honored every time and easy to claim.

  • Maaasahan

    Dependable. Clear routes, posted schedules and enough vehicles at rush hour, so a commute is something you can plan around.

  • Para sa lahat

    Accessible. Covered walkways, ramps, working elevators and priority seats. A sidewalk is part of the route.

  • Pakinggan

    Heard. Commuters have a seat at the table when routes, fares and projects are decided, and complaints get an answer.

Isang araw ng commuter

An example day, written to show where each of the five needs goes unmet. It is not one person’s record.

  1. 4:30 AM

    Wake up and leave early, because no one can say when the first ride will arrive.
    Maaasahan

  2. 5:20 AM

    Walk to the terminal on a road with no sidewalk and no streetlight.
    Ligtas

  3. 5:45 AM

    Line up. The jeep fills before you reach the front, and the fare takes a big share of the day’s pay.
    Abot-kaya

  4. 7:10 AM

    Transfer to the train. The platform is packed, so you let two trains go.
    Maaasahan

  5. 8:20 AM

    Climb an uncovered footbridge with no ramp, with a heavy bag, in the heat or the rain.
    Para sa lahat

  6. 8:50 AM

    Arrive at work or class already tired. Nobody asked how the ride was.
    Pakinggan

  7. 7:30 PM

    Start the trip home. The same queues, now in the dark and sometimes in the rain.
    Ligtas

  8. 9:15 PM

    Home. The alarm is set for 4:30.

Ano ang magagawa mo

  • Ikuwento

    Share your commute. Tell family, classmates and coworkers what your ride is really like. Real stories move people more than numbers.

  • Isumbong

    Report what you see. Note broken lights, unsafe stops, overcharging and long waits, then raise them with your barangay, your city hall, or agencies such as the LTFRB and DOTr.

  • Sumali

    Join the conversation. Attend public consultations and hearings on routes and fares when they are announced, and bring other commuters with you.

Para po. Para sa commuter. Ligtas, abot-kaya at maaasahang biyahe para sa lahat. #ParaPoSaCommuter
